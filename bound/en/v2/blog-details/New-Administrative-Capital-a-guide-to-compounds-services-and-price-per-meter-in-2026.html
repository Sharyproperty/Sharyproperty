The New Administrative Capital is the largest urban project Egypt has built in recent years, and it has become a prime destination for anyone looking for a modern home or a long-term real estate investment. This guide covers where the capital is and how to get there, its main residential districts, the best compounds, the services available, the average price per meter and payment plans, plus practical tips before you buy.

Read also: get to know New Cairo and the New Administrative Capital, follow the Shary Index for price per metre, and browse units for sale and top developers.

Where is the New Administrative Capital?

The New Administrative Capital lies east of Cairo, about 45 km from the city centre, between the Cairo–Suez and Cairo–Ain Sokhna roads, close to New Cairo, Madinaty, El Shorouk and Badr City. Its total planned area is around 170,000 feddans, developed in phases.

How to get to the New Capital

  • Mohamed Bin Zayed axis (north and south)
  • Cairo–Suez road and Cairo–Ain Sokhna road
  • The Middle Ring Road and the Regional Ring Road
  • The Light Rail Transit (LRT) from Adly Mansour station
  • The East Nile monorail from Nasr City through New Cairo

Residential districts in the New Capital

Phase one includes eight residential districts, labelled R1 to R8, alongside the Government District, the Central Business District and the Diplomatic District. These are the districts buyers ask about most:

  • Third Residential District (R3) Home to the state-built “Capital Residence” project, and one of the first districts to be lived in.
  • Fifth Residential District (R5) Known as “New Garden City”, with wide green spaces and mid-rise buildings.
  • Seventh Residential District (R7) The most in-demand district, with many private developers’ compounds near the Green River and the Diplomatic District.
  • Eighth Residential District (R8) A newer district with projects by major developers, close to the embassies area and the main roads.

Services and facilities in the New Capital

The capital was planned as a fully serviced city, so residents do not need to leave it for their daily needs:

  • Education International and private universities, such as the German International University and the Universities of Canada in Egypt, plus international and language schools inside the residential districts.
  • Business The Government District and the Central Business District, home to the Iconic Tower, the tallest tower in Africa, and the headquarters of banks and companies.
  • Leisure The Green River parks, the City of Arts and Culture, Egypt International Olympic City, shopping centres and restaurants.
  • Transport The Light Rail Transit and the monorail, with a network of wide roads linking the districts.

Payment and installment plans

Most developers in the New Capital offer flexible payment plans, with a down payment that usually starts from 5% to 10% of the unit price and the balance paid in installments over up to 8 or 10 years. Terms differ from one project to another, so compare the total price, the down payment and the delivery date, not just the installment amount.

Pros and cons of living in the New Capital

  • Modern planning Wide streets, green spaces and smart infrastructure designed from scratch.
  • Full services Education, healthcare, business and leisure inside the city itself.
  • Variety of units Apartments, duplexes, villas, and office and retail units in different sizes and payment plans.
  • Distance from central Cairo Still relatively far for anyone whose work is in downtown or West Cairo.
  • Uneven occupancy Some areas are still under construction, so check the build progress and actual occupancy around the unit.

Tips before buying a unit in the New Capital

  1. Review the developer’s track record and how well it met delivery dates in earlier projects.
  2. Check the ministerial decree and project licences, and visit the site to see the actual build progress.
  3. Read the contract carefully: delivery date, delay penalties and the maintenance deposit.
  4. Compare the average price per meter in the same district before negotiating the price.
  5. Be clear about your goal: living needs closeness to services, while investment needs a location with resale or rental demand.

Frequently asked questions about the New Administrative Capital

How far is the New Administrative Capital from Cairo?

It is about 45 km from central Cairo, and minutes from New Cairo via the Mohamed Bin Zayed axis.

Which residential district is best in the New Capital?

R7 and R8 are the most in demand among developers’ projects, while R3 suits buyers who want ready units in an inhabited area.

Can you reach the New Capital by public transport?

Yes: by the Light Rail Transit from Adly Mansour station, the East Nile monorail from Nasr City, and bus lines.

Is the New Capital a good real estate investment?

It suits long-term investors who choose a reliable developer and a location close to services and main roads, after checking build progress and average prices.

How do I find the current average price per meter in the New Capital?

From the Shary Index, which shows the updated average price per meter for each area and unit type, and the rate of change.
